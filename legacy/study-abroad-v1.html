Why Mechatronics

Where the software kept hitting a wall

01

Sondhan can help someone find a blood donor — but it cannot verify blood quality at the point of use. That needs sensing, not a database query.

02

Bazare and Healdi can route a delivery — but neither can sense the condition of what it's carrying in transit. That needs embedded hardware, not an API call.

03

Every one of these systems ends at the screen. Mechatronics — mechanical systems, electronics, embedded programming, sensors, and control — is the foundation for building systems that sense and act in the physical world, not just process and display information.

Statement of Purpose

The full statement

I am most motivated by a simple question: can I turn a real problem into something that works? Rather than being interested in technology only as a subject of study, I have gradually developed a habit of learning by building. When an idea interests me, I try to understand the problem, learn the technologies I need, develop a solution, and improve it through experimentation. This approach has led me from building software products to a broader ambition: developing intelligent physical systems that can solve problems in the real world. Mechatronics Engineering is the next step I need to pursue that ambition.

I completed my Dakhil (Secondary School Certificate) in 2022 from Ahmadia Kamil Madrasa, Hajiganj, Chandpur, and my Higher Secondary Certificate from Dhaka College in 2024, both with a GPA of 5.00 out of 5.00 in the Science group. I have also achieved an IELTS Academic overall score of 6.5. Alongside my academic education, I have consistently pursued activities that have developed different aspects of my abilities. From an early age, I trained in music and participated in cultural competitions, achieving first place at the district level on several occasions and performing on television. Music taught me discipline, persistence, confidence, and the importance of continuous practice — qualities that have also shaped the way I approach my technical work.

My strongest practical learning, however, has come from building products. My flagship project, Sondhan, is a live blood donor search platform developed around a real and time-sensitive problem in Bangladesh. I designed and developed the platform to help users find potential blood donors based on blood group and location, while also considering donor privacy and system reliability. Taking Sondhan from an idea to a functioning product taught me that building technology for real users is fundamentally different from completing a classroom assignment. Production bugs, deployment issues, user experience, reliability, and continuous improvement all became part of the responsibility of building a real product.

That experience encouraged me to continue experimenting. I have developed or worked on products including Bazare, an e-commerce platform; MOVE, a gamified running and fitness concept; Furqan, a Quran-understanding platform for Bengali-speaking users; DeterMind, a student-oriented self-improvement platform; and Sigma, an AI-based system that I have been developing. These projects cover different problems and technologies, but they reflect one consistent pattern in how I learn: I start with an idea, identify what I do not know, acquire the necessary knowledge, build a solution, and learn from what happens when that solution meets reality.

Over time, this process has revealed a limitation in what I can currently create. Almost everything I have built exists digitally. Software can process information, connect people, automate workflows, and deliver intelligent services, but many of the problems that interest me require a system to physically sense its environment, make decisions, move, measure, or act.

That realization is what led me to Mechatronics Engineering.

I see mechatronics as the bridge between the digital and physical worlds. It brings together mechanical systems, electronics, programming, sensors, embedded systems, control, automation, and robotics. This multidisciplinary foundation is exactly what I need to expand from building software products to building intelligent machines and physical products. I want to understand not only how to write the code behind a system, but also how the sensors, electronics, mechanical structures, actuators, and control mechanisms work together to make that system function.

One example of the kind of problem I want to explore is aquaculture. I have been thinking about a small fish-shaped robotic device that could operate in water and collect information about water quality, environmental conditions, and fish activity. I have not built this device, and I do not present it as an existing achievement. Instead, it represents the kind of problem I want to become technically capable of solving. Turning such an idea into a reliable product would require knowledge of sensors, embedded systems, electronics, mechanical design, control, communication, and eventually intelligent data processing. This is precisely the type of multidisciplinary challenge that attracts me to Mechatronics.

What I want from the program

During my undergraduate studies, I want to build a rigorous foundation in engineering mathematics, physics, mechanics, electronics, programming, embedded systems, sensors and instrumentation, control systems, CAD, robotics, and automation. More importantly, I want to connect this knowledge with continuous practical experimentation. My goal is to graduate not only having passed engineering courses, but having developed the ability to move from a problem to a prototype and from a prototype toward a viable product.

Long-term direction

My long-term ambition extends beyond a conventional engineering career. I want to become a product-oriented engineer and technology entrepreneur. I intend to identify meaningful problems, develop innovative solutions, build and test early prototypes myself, and eventually create multidisciplinary teams capable of turning those solutions into scalable products. I want to build technology companies that address problems at meaningful scale and, over the course of my career, aspire to create globally relevant businesses capable of influencing entire industries.

I understand that ambitious products and companies are not created by ambition alone. They require technical depth, disciplined execution, experimentation, resilience, teamwork, and the ability to learn continuously. My experience in software development has already taught me how to begin that process. My education in Mechatronics will give me the engineering foundation to take it much further.

I therefore do not see Mechatronics Engineering simply as a degree that will lead to a job. I see it as the foundation for the kind of builder I want to become: someone capable of identifying real problems, understanding the technology required to solve them, creating the first working version, and eventually leading teams that turn those solutions into products with global impact. I have learned how to turn ideas into digital products. Now I want to learn how to turn ideas into intelligent machines — and ultimately build companies around solutions that matter.
